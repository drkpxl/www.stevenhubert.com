Corduroy is the design system my team built out across Alterra's digital surfaces. It powers the 19 resort websites, the Ikon Pass marketing site, the Ikon AMP commerce app, and the Ikon Pass mobile app. The name is a nod to freshly groomed trails, which felt right for a system whose whole job is consistency over a surface area that keeps growing.

The goal we set for it was simple: flexible enough that each resort brand keeps its individuality, structured enough that the whole network feels like one digital experience. That tension is the actual work. A system that locks every team down ships fast and feels generic. A system that lets every team improvise ships slow and feels inconsistent. Corduroy lives in the middle on purpose.

![Token flow diagram from the Corduroy leadership deck](https://placehold.co/1200x675?text=Token+flow+diagram)
*Tokens centralize design values in one place, so a color or font change happens once and reflects across every product*

It is built around three layers: Foundations, Components, and Documentation. Tokens carry the design values so a color or type change happens in one place and reflects across every product. Per-resort themes layer on top so the same booking widget or comparison chart picks up the right look when it lands on Deer Valley versus Crystal Mountain. Documentation sits beside both so any designer, engineer, or agency partner can find the right pattern without asking.

![Per-resort themes applied to one component](https://placehold.co/1200x675?text=Per-resort+theme+comparison)
*The same component, themed for two different resorts via tokens*

The value to Alterra is concrete. New resorts and new products ship faster because the team is not redrawing buttons. Internal teams and external agencies share a vocabulary, so handoffs cost less. Most importantly, the team's design hours go into improving the guest experience instead of making repeat decisions about borders and spacing.
