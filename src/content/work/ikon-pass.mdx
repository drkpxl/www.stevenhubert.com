Leading the Ikon Pass website for five consecutive seasons taught me how hard it is to keep a homepage from collapsing into a price sheet as the shop grows. My team owned design, UX, digital product, and content strategy for the site. Marketing set the brand and the campaign each year. My job was to translate that into a shop and a homepage that held up as the product matrix kept expanding around it.

The site also inherited the UX and product lessons of two pass products my team had already shipped; the Rocky Mountain Super Pass and the MAX Pass.

![Ikon Pass Homepage Evolution](/images/Homepage.png)
*Ikon Pass Homepage Evolution*

Each season I refreshed the homepage to match the year's marketing moment. Some years the headline was a new app, some years a specific partner story. As the partner roster grew, the framing had to shift away from counting mountains and toward the texture of the experience itself.

![Ikon Pass Shop Passes Evolution](/images/ShopPasses.png)
*Ikon Pass Shop Passes Evolution*

As the product matrix grew, the design work became as much about merchandising as it was about layout. Some products carried more weight than others, and the shop had to surface the heroes without hiding the ones that served a smaller audience. Base Pass Plus is the clearest example. Never the volume seller, but real to the audience it served. We didn't want to bury it in the sitemap, so the visual system gave it presence without letting it pull focus from the happy path.

![Ikon Pass Shop Passes Sales Block](/images/IkonShopPasses23.png)
*Ikon Pass Shop Passes Sales Block*

A few small moves carry most of the load in the layout above: a height hierarchy that reads tallest to shortest, a clear left-to-right ordering, a yellow feature ball that calls out the top-tier benefit, and a compact icon set that absorbs the cognitive weight of explaining a no-blackout product.

Five seasons in, the design system has held. New products, new partners, new campaigns; the shop and the homepage absorbed all of it without breaking the patterns the team built.
