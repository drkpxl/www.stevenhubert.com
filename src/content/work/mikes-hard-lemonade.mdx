I executive produced a year of social campaigns for Mike's Hard at Grey NY. The brief was to give Mike's a real voice in social on a budget that was a fraction of what the bigger beer brands were spending. A dozen swings followed; the one I still think about is Mike's Mariachi.

### Mike's Mariachi

For Cuatro de Mayo we commissioned a local Brooklyn band, dressed them as Mariachis, and had them play songs written in real time by a team of copywriters working from live tweets.

![Mike's Mariachi](/images/Mariachi+Live+Stream.jpg)
*Mike's Mariachi Live Stream*

The interesting production problem was getting lyrics written, legally cleared, and onto the teleprompter fast enough to keep a live stream live. The [workflow](/images/workflow.png) ran tweets through a Google doc, a copywriter, a Creative Director, Legal, and into the teleprompter for the band. The live show ran clean. The campaign was a breakout for the brand and pulled press from [AgencySpy](http://www.mediabistro.com/agencyspy/mikes-hard-lemonade-band-will-mariachi-ize-just-about-any-song_b32710), [Ad Week](http://www.adweek.com/adfreak/mikes-hard-lemonade-welcomes-some-unexpected-visitors-142143), and others.

<Video poster="/images/mm1.jpg" src="/videos/MikesMariachi.mp4" />

The other Mike's work that year ran in the same register: a Labor Day VIP-experience sweepstakes called Bear in a Bikini, a Facebook soundboard app called Score the Fourth, and a string of one-off social moments. Small budget, recognizable brand presence, real audience.
