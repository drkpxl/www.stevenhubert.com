Jabber is a family conversation starter. You set a phone on the dinner table, tap the screen, and one question shows up. Then you talk about it. No accounts, no scores, no leaderboard, no return-visit prompt. When you are done you put the phone down.

![A Jabber question card](https://placehold.co/1200x675?text=Jabber+question+card)
*A typical Jabber question: "What's the most peaceful place you can think of?"*

I built it because my own family kept slipping into the same "how was school" routine and I wanted something that nudged us out of it without becoming another piece of software my kids were asking for. The question set is tuned for families with kids between four and twelve, but it works for almost anyone who wants better dinner conversation. Adult dinners with friends use it too.

The technical brief is intentionally short. A curated set of questions, a button, a deep link per question so a good one can be shared by URL. No ads. No monetization. No telemetry. The point of the product is that nothing about it tries to keep you in the app.

The build is also a useful counter-example for me when projects at work start collecting analytics for analytics' sake. Sometimes the right answer is to ship something that respects its audience and walks away.
