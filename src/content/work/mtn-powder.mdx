Mountain Powder grew out of a problem the snow-reporting team brought to me. Every morning, reporters across six resorts logged into multiple systems to update conditions for the website, the email, the trail signs, and a half-dozen other surfaces. The data lived in too many places, and most of the tooling only worked on a narrow set of desktop browsers.

I led the product end-to-end. The pitch was one responsive admin panel as the single source of truth for snow, lift, trail, activity, and weather data, with that data piped out everywhere. The harder problem turned out to be getting six resorts to agree on a shared vocabulary, not the interface itself.

![Winter Park's Snow Reporting in Tablet View](/images/QTA/Screenshot%202017-05-05%2010.15.55.png)
*Snow Reporting view, tablet-optimized for use at the base of the mountain*

Reporters override the live weather feed when reality on the mountain disagrees with the sensors. Stations freeze with rime ice and report 32° in a storm when it's actually -10°; an anemometer seizes and reads 0 mph during an 80 mph gust; an animal cuts the feed. The override is what lets reporters keep guests accurate while the hardware catches up.

Every override and edit writes to an audit log. Before Mountain Powder, each change was printed and filed in a cabinet so legal could prove what had been published when.

![Overrides](/images/QTA/Screenshot%202017-05-05%2010.38.27.png)
*Override Options, with full audit logging*

The same JSON spine drives the customer-facing operations panel at each resort, the daily snow-report email through IBM Watson Marketing Cloud, the digital signage in the lodges, and a custom Alexa skill for resort conditions.

![Alert Signup](/images/QTA/Screenshot%202017-05-05.png)
*Text Alert Signup at alerts.quicktrax.com*

The last layer is a text-alert signup. Pass holders pick thresholds and get a one-way message when one trips. I worked with legal to wall this list off from every other marketing surface we operate; numbers entered here never get marketed to, and the channel has held its integrity every season since.

Mountain Powder started as a tool to give a reporter back half an hour every morning. It became the data spine that feeds almost every customer-facing surface at every Alterra resort.

### Recent redesign

Five years in, the team and I took Mountain Powder apart and put it back together. The reporting interface, the admin tooling, and the downstream pipeline all got rebuilt around what the product actually has to do now versus what we shipped it to do in 2020. The product had grown beyond its original brief, and the original architecture was starting to feel it.
