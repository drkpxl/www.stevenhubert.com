Ski Colorado was a multi-mountain pass product anchored on the Rocky Mountain Super Pass+, and the site selling it was overdue for a rebuild ([archive of the old version here](http://bit.ly/2nK78AP)). The old site tried to sell our full pass portfolio at once. The analytics were clear that the RSMP+ was the hero and deserved hero treatment.

We rebuilt around that single product. Sections were dedicated and easy to scan, and the FAQ answered the questions Guest Services at Winter Park reported hearing most. Initial heat mapping and NPS responses came back encouraging.

![SkiColorado Heatmap](/images/p1372.jpeg)

On the platform side we went static. [Jekyll](https://jekyllrb.com/) for build, [Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/) for hosting, the same stack we'd been using on the more dynamic [CMH](https://www.canadianmountainholidays.com/) site.

![Pingdom Results](/images/pingdom.jpg)

That is an "A" Pingdom rating on an image-heavy site with real JavaScript driving the interactions. Not a trivial result.

For the marketing team we wired in [Forestry](https://forestry.io/) as a lightweight CMS so anyone could edit copy, rates, and FAQs without pulling in a developer. We also layered in [Convert](https://www.convert.com/) for A/B testing and personalization, so Texas customers see different messaging than Colorado or New York customers, and CTAs and color get tested in flight using patterns that had already moved conversion on [TheMaxPass.com](https://www.themaxpass.com/).

The part I'm most proud of: this was designed, built, written, and optimized in-house. An agency engagement would have cost a multiple of what we spent, and the resulting site would likely not have performed as well or shipped as fast. Worth doing again on the next scope that fits.
