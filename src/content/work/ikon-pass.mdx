Leading the Ikon Pass website for five consecutive seasons taught me how hard it is to keep a homepage from collapsing into a price sheet as the shop grows. My team owned design, UX, digital product, and content strategy. Marketing set the brand and the campaign each year; my job was to translate that into a shop and a homepage that held up while the product matrix kept expanding around it.

Ikon also inherited the UX and product lessons of two pass products my team had already shipped: the Rocky Mountain Super Pass and the MAX Pass.

![Ikon Pass Homepage Evolution](/images/Homepage.png)
*Ikon Pass Homepage Evolution*

Each season I refreshed the homepage to match the year's marketing moment. As the partner roster grew, the framing had to shift away from counting mountains and toward the texture of the experience itself.

As the product matrix grew, the design work became as much about merchandising as it was about layout. Some products carried more weight than others, and the shop had to surface the heroes without hiding the smaller-audience products like Base Pass Plus.

A few small moves carry most of the load in the shop layout: a height hierarchy that reads tallest to shortest, left-to-right ordering, a yellow feature ball that calls out the top-tier benefit, and a compact icon set that absorbs the cognitive weight of explaining a no-blackout product.

Five seasons in, the design system has held. New products, new partners, new campaigns; the shop and the homepage absorbed all of it without breaking the patterns the team built.
