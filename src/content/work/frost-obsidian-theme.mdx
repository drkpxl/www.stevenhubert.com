I keep most of my professional notes in Obsidian. Every theme I tried felt either too busy or too dated, so I built Frost, a clean theme inspired by Tailwind defaults and Apple Notes. It is now in the official Obsidian community theme marketplace and is the daily driver for my notes.

![Frost theme preview in dark and light](https://placehold.co/1200x675?text=Frost+theme+dark+and+light)
*Frost in dark and light, with a default accent color*

The design choices are quiet on purpose. Generous line height. SF Pro Display for headings, Atkinson Hyperlegible for body, JetBrains Mono for code. Light and dark modes that share the same structural rhythm so switching does not break my reading flow. A configurable accent color for users who want one personal touch without rebuilding the theme.

It is open source under MIT and lives at [github.com/drkpxl/Frost](https://github.com/drkpxl/Frost). Submitting it to the official Obsidian marketplace meant working through the same kind of contribution process I ask of vendors at work, which was a useful empathy-building exercise.

It is a small thing in the grand scheme. I keep it on the portfolio because it is a piece of finished design work I get to live inside every day, and because the choices I made on it (typography, restraint, the single accent control) are the same choices I argue for in much larger systems.
