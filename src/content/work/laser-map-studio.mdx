Laser Map Studio started as a tool I wanted to use myself. There were already a few ways to turn OpenStreetMap data into a laser-engraved wooden map of your city, but every one of them either required hours of cleanup in a CAD program or produced files that were not actually manifold and failed on the print bed. I built Laser Map Studio to handle both ends of that pipeline for makers who care about the finished object, not the geospatial plumbing under it.

![Laser Map Studio main interface](https://placehold.co/1200x675?text=Studio+UI)
*The studio: pick a location, toggle road, building, and water layers, and export to the format your machine wants*

The output side covers SVG for laser cutters, DXF for CAD, and 3MF for multi-material 3D printers, including the layered files needed for terrain elevation or stacked-material designs. The customization side lets makers tune road tiers, building heights, water rendering, and the bits that actually matter for a clean cut or a clean print. Sample work includes laser-cut wooden maps of Downtown Denver and Lower Manhattan and resin and PETG terrain prints from the same source data.

![Sample maker output from the studio](https://placehold.co/1200x675?text=Showcase+gallery)
*Sample output: wooden map of Lower Manhattan and 3D-printed terrain in resin*

I built it in Python on top of a custom processing engine that consumes OpenStreetMap data, and run subscriptions through Polar. The whole thing taught me to operate a real product end-to-end at small scale: billing, customer support, release notes, a Discord community. Those are exactly the muscles I want sharp before applying them to the larger surfaces I lead at Alterra.
