Executive case note / portfolio operating system

Mountain Powder

I turned fragmented resort condition reporting into a shared operating layer for Alterra. The design work was not a screen. It was the system of decisions around it.

Role Conceived, led, shipped, and evolved
Scope Product strategy, UX, data model, ops tooling
Business surface Every resort condition channel
Operating value Fewer tools, cleaner data, auditable truth
Leadership readout 2017-present

Portfolio leverage, not feature polish.

Mountain Powder matters because it changed how operational data moves through a multi-resort company. It gave teams one vocabulary, one publishing path, and one accountable record of what guests saw.

Problem Reporters were re-entering condition data across disconnected tools.
Decision Make reporting the source of truth, then push structured data downstream.
Result A daily operational dependency that kept expanding instead of being replaced.

01 / VP lens

The value was in the operating choices.

The interface mattered, but the durable work was alignment, governance, exception handling, and trust boundaries across teams. Those choices turned a resort-by-resort reporting problem into a company operating layer.

Leadership area
Decision
Why it mattered
What changed

Shared vocabulary

Get resorts onto common definitions for snow, lifts, trails, activities, and weather.

The hard problem was not the form. It was making six operational cultures publish comparable data.

One schema could feed many guest surfaces without each resort becoming a special case.

Exception design

Treat manual overrides as first-class workflow, not a workaround.

Mountain sensors fail in exactly the moments guests care about: storms, ice, wind, and outages.

Reporters could correct reality while preserving the machine feed and the reason for the edit.

Governance

Record every edit and override in an audit trail.

Legal needed proof of what had been published and when, without printed binders.

The system became safer to operate because accountability was built into the product.

Channel trust

Wall SMS alerts off from the broader marketing list.

A powder alert only works if guests believe the number will not become another campaign target.

The alert channel kept its integrity season after season.

02 / Operating model

One source of truth, many downstream obligations.

The product sits between messy operational reality and polished guest-facing surfaces. Its job is to absorb disagreement, preserve accountability, and publish a clean signal.

Input Resort ops

Local mountain reality

Reporters combine observed conditions, lift and trail status, live station feeds, and human judgement.

  • Snow report
  • Lift and trail status
  • Weather station feed
  • Manual override
Core Product system

Shared schema plus accountability layer

Responsive admin, normalized data, permissions, audit history, and a publishing model built for resort-scale variation.

  • Single reporter-facing surface
  • JSON backbone
  • Edit and override audit log
  • Redesigned around mature workflows
Output Guest channels

Consistent condition signal

The same source feeds every surface where guests make decisions before or during a mountain day.

  • Operations panel
  • Daily snow email
  • Lodge signage
  • SMS threshold alerts
  • Alexa skill

03 / Product evidence

Operational surfaces made the system accountable.

The product had to work in real operational contexts: tablet use at the base of the mountain, overrides during bad weather, guest alerts, and public signage. The UI was only valuable if it made those situations faster and safer.

  • Admin Designed around fast morning reporting rather than a marketing CMS model.
  • Signage Public proof that the internal data spine reached the guest experience.
  • Alerts A trust-sensitive channel with boundaries designed into the product policy.

04 / Outcomes

The result was a durable operating asset.

The leverage shows up as less duplication, clearer accountability, stronger guest trust, and a platform that could absorb growth without a restart.

All resorts The system moved from initial resort coverage to a portfolio-wide condition reporting backbone.
~30 min Returned to each reporter in the morning workflow that had been split across tools.
100% Edits and overrides audit-logged so the company can prove what guests saw.
7+ years Production life with a redesign around maturity rather than a costly replacement cycle.

The product became institutional memory.

Mountain Powder started as a tool to give a reporter back half an hour every morning. It became the data spine behind guest-facing operations, alerting, signage, snow email, and the audit record the business depends on.
