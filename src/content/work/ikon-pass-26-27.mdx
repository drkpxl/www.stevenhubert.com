For the 26/27 Ikon Pass on-sale my team rebuilt the most-trafficked surfaces of the site. The homepage gained a new comparison chart and lost the standalone Shop Passes page entirely. A new refundable product variant moved into top billing. The checkout path lost about four clicks from where it had been the year before.

It was the largest year-over-year change to the site I have shipped in five seasons of leading this work, and the bar for the change was high. Marketing was launching the Find Joy campaign at the same time, Ischgl was joining the destination roster, and the team was rolling out Peak Perks, First Tracks, and renewal benefits, each of which needed a place to live in the shop.

![Ikon Pass 26/27 homepage comparison chart](https://placehold.co/1200x675?text=Homepage+comparison+chart)
*The new homepage comparison chart, which absorbed the work the old Shop Passes page used to do*

Most of the value of the new homepage chart is that a returning passholder can see, in one scroll, what is different this year and which variant fits them. That was the question Guest Services was answering most often, and pulling it onto the homepage took both the question and a click out of the funnel.

![Checkout flow before and after](https://placehold.co/1200x675?text=Checkout+before+%2F+after)
*Before / after of the checkout flow, condensed by roughly four clicks*

The change took the site from a price-sheet posture back to a product-and-experience posture, which is the right place for it to sit as the network keeps growing.
