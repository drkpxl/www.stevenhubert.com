This product came out of a conversation with a Winter Park ski patroller named Will. Ski patrol communication today usually runs over phone or CB radio. The patroller on scene calls a supervisor, who calls A, B, and C depending on the situation. Will's idea was to expand the QTA platform with private messaging groups that could send one-way alerts at each stage of an incident escalation.

An injury escalates differently than a mechanical issue, and a personnel issue follows a different path again. Letting patrol define custom groups makes those escalation paths cleaner and faster under pressure.

Because the use case is urgent, the UI does as little as possible. Three sections: add recipients, create or edit groups, send a message. That's it. The product is not novel and there are SaaS tools in this space, but those tools were not shaped around a ski resort's operational reality or its budget. A modest Heroku bill plus the per-message cost was enough to run this at a real resort.

QuickTrax SOS rolled out across the Intrawest family: Winter Park, Steamboat, Stratton, Blue, Tremblant, and Snowshoe. The natural next step is two-way communication, an inbox the right people can monitor, but a 24/7 inbox needs a clear ownership story before it ships. Options on the table:

- Forwarding to a specific set of numbers responsible for monitoring
- Browser push notifications
- Email alerts, or a native lightweight client similar to Slack but trimmed down for this single purpose
