Frost is a clean Obsidian theme I designed and ship as open source. It is in the official Obsidian community marketplace and is my daily driver for notes. Calling it a small project undersells the point of having it on this site: senior designers rarely have public artifacts to back up the typography and restraint choices they argue for in larger systems. Frost is mine.

![Frost theme preview in dark and light](https://placehold.co/1200x675?text=Frost+theme+dark+and+light)
*Frost in dark and light, with a default accent color*

The brief was clear. Lead with typography, not chrome. SF Pro Display for headings, Atkinson Hyperlegible for body, JetBrains Mono for code, picked deliberately rather than reaching for whatever the platform default was. Generous line height. A single configurable accent color so users can personalize without rebuilding the theme. Dark and light modes that share the same structural rhythm, so switching modes does not break the reader's flow.

The harder discipline was deciding what not to include. Most other themes I had tried were loud in some direction. Frost is what is left after cutting that.

Shipping it through Obsidian's community theme review was a useful empathy exercise. Every contribution pipeline I run at work (design tokens, brand reviews, accessibility checks) is a friction point for someone trying to ship. Sitting on the other side of one for a public open-source artifact gave me a sharper read on what actually serves the contributor and what is gatekeeping for its own sake.

I keep Frost on this portfolio because the design choices on it are the same ones I argue for in much larger systems at Alterra. The difference is that here you can look at them, install them, and decide for yourself.
